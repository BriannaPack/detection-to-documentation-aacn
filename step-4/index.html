Make your students SHOW their work

A group project set up with SHOW so the revision history captures every student’s writing from the first draft.

Now try it

Think of a writing assignment in one of your courses where authenticity has been a concern. How might you redesign it using the SHOW approach?

As you plan, consider each part:

S

Structure the assignment

Break it into milestones in one shared Doc, and spell out the rules in the assignment itself.

H

Hold students accountable

Check the revision history at the first milestone, and follow through on your rules.

O

Observe and respond

Plan where you’ll give feedback, and have students revise in the same Doc so the history shows their growth.

W

Weigh the whole picture

Add the process to your rubric, such as participation and revision, not just the final product.

Sample assignment language you can paste in
  1. Add me as an editor when you create this Google Doc. I use the revision history to verify participation and authorship and ensure fairness. Students who do not show active involvement in the Google Doc will not receive credit.
  2. Do all writing directly in the Google Doc. The revision history is how I see your writing process, and the process is part of what this assignment asks for. Content that is written elsewhere and pasted, imported, or uploaded into the document cannot be verified, so it will receive a zero.

How to submit (follow these steps in order):

  1. Create a new Google Doc. Title it LastName_FirstName_AssignmentName.
  2. Share it with your faculty as Editor. Click the blue Share button, enter your faculty’s email, change the role from Viewer to Editor, and click Send. Viewer or Commenter access will prevent grading and will be treated as a missed submission.
  3. Submit the URL in Canvas. Paste the link into the text-entry box. Confirm it works by opening it in a private browser window.
  4. Write directly in the Google Doc. Do all drafting, revising, and analysis in this document from start to finish.

Important: Do not upload a Word or PDF copy; only the shared Google Doc link is accepted. Your instructor grades in the Google Doc and reviews the revision history to confirm authorship. Large copied, pasted, or imported content, including text from another document, a website, or an AI tool, will receive a grade of 0. Do not change sharing permissions after the deadline.

Submission steps adapted from Sharon Titus’s teaching statement assignment.

Know the limits

  • Pasting from elsewhere. Students can draft in another file and paste it in, so some authorship can’t be verified. Treat a paste as a reason to ask, and set the expectation at the first milestone.
  • Internet access. Composing in Google Docs needs a steady connection, which can disadvantage some students. Build in flexible milestones.
  • Infrastructure. Google Workspace and Chrome extensions aren’t standard in every nursing program. The SHOW approach still works with any tool that keeps a version history.
